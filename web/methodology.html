Methodology

The protocol ACBS follows, why it is shaped this way, and where its results should be read with care. Modeled on the OpenML AutoML Benchmark (AMLB).

1. Identical conditions

  • Every framework trains on the same stratified folds, same hold-out split.
  • One seed (default 42) drives splits, models and search.
  • One wall-clock budget per framework per fold, plus a grace period; the runner kills the whole process tree when it expires.
  • Core and memory limits are passed through to each environment.

2. Central scoring

  • Frameworks return predictions only — never their own score.
  • ACBS computes Accuracy, Precision, Recall, F1, ROC-AUC from stored predictions.
  • Binary problems are judged by the positive-class probability; multiclass by the per-class matrix.
  • A constant-predictor floor scores every dataset, so a "good" number has a baseline.

3. Failures are results

  • Each run ends as ok, failed, timeout or skipped — recorded, never dropped.
  • One framework crashing cannot take the benchmark with it: it runs in its own subprocess and environment.
  • The failure count is always visible next to the ranking.

4. Ranking & recommendation

  • Metrics are normalised and combined with your weights (weighted sum or TOPSIS).
  • Optional constraints (minimum recall, maximum training time) exclude models before ranking — excluded models stay visible.
  • Stability is measured by perturbing the weights ±20% and re-ranking.
  • Pareto analysis of score vs training time shows the efficiency frontier.

Budget ladder

Ranks correlate strongly across budgets (the AMLB analysis reports >0.96 between 30 and 60 minutes), so short budgets are a legitimate choice: ACBS offers 1 / 5 / 15 / 30 minute presets, and short budgets automatically shrink per-framework search populations where a framework's own timer would otherwise overrun its wall-clock limit.

Caveats & limitations

  • H2O requires a JVM; NaiveAutoML is experimental (AMLB excluded it from its published analysis).
  • AutoML search can be non-deterministic even with fixed seeds; parallel libraries may use slightly different thread counts per environment.
  • Default here: 3 folds (AMLB uses 10) — a documented, honest reduction for local compute budgets.
  • The recommendation is only as good as the weights you give it; re-rank freely.
